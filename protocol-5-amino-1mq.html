5-Amino-1MQ NNMT inhibitor (small molecule)

5-Amino-1MQ is a small, cell-permeable molecule studied as a selective inhibitor of the enzyme NNMT (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase). Research interest centers on metabolic effects such as fat oxidation, preservation of lean mass, and raising intracellular NAD+.

Dosage Chart (10 mg vial)

Reconstitute with 2 mL bacteriostatic water → 5,000 mcg/mL.

Frequency: Once or twice daily, subcutaneous · Half-life: ~4-7 hours

How it works

NNMT consumes nicotinamide and methyl groups; when it is overexpressed in fat tissue it can lower NAD+ availability. By blocking NNMT, 5-Amino-1MQ is thought to spare nicotinamide for NAD+ synthesis and support downstream sirtuin (SIRT1) activity linked to mitochondrial function.

Reconstitution steps

  1. Let the vial reach room temperature (~15-20 min).
  2. Draw 2 mL bacteriostatic water and inject slowly down the vial wall.
  3. Swirl gently until clear; do not shake.
  4. Label, refrigerate (2-8°C), protect from light; use within a few weeks.

Potential benefits

  • Studied for reduced fat mass while sparing lean muscle in preclinical models.
  • Associated with higher NAD+ levels and SIRT1 pathway activity in research settings.
  • Generally well tolerated in early studies; long-term human data are not established.

Notes

  • Investigational compound; human safety data are limited.
  • A mild stinging sensation on injection has been reported.
  • Morning dosing is common; twice-daily splits track the short half-life.
